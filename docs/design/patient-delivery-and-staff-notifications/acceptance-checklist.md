# CareIQ notifications: edge cases and acceptance

Design review · October 18, 2026 · Synthetic data only

These are implementation acceptance scenarios, not checks executed against a live delivery provider. The design task does not activate delivery, subscribe webhooks or contact patients or staff. The primary handoff and backend contract define the chosen defaults; this checklist identifies behavior that must remain observable and testable.

## Preserve the established model

- A request, delivery intent, actual provider attempt, delivery observation, patient action, completed document and staff review are different facts. No single “sent/completed” badge represents them all.
- One patient belongs to one clinical location in the current release. An assignment or notification never grants clinical access. Practice ownership alone does not grant clinical location access.
- The newer inspected implementation baseline limits the patient portal to currently eligible adult-self access. This notification release preserves that gate. Proxy/minor scenarios below verify safe denial or an explicitly approved future authority model; they do not authorize expanding the launch audience.
- Patient signing finishes only after all required signers and durable verified output/evidence. A patient signature while a provider signature is pending is not completed paperwork. Form-only submission produces an unsigned submitted copy and truthful copy.
- The patient library references the exact immutable released version. Notification failure cannot undo completion or remove the authorized in-app copy.
- A staff notification is a personal attention record attached to source work. Work status remains authoritative; reading a notification never resolves its source work.

## Patient delivery acceptance

| ID | Scenario | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | Send an initial request, a manual reminder and a completed-copy notice. | Each has an explicit purpose, template version, intended recipient, channel and destination version. The request and its attempts remain separately inspectable. A reminder does not create a second request. |
| P02 | Provider is missing, disabled or unavailable. | Return a truthful unavailable/queued/failed result as applicable. Never label it sent or delivered. The request can remain open for supported assistance; the in-app completed copy remains available. |
| P03 | Email or phone is merely entered by staff, malformed, unverified, restricted or known permanently undeliverable. | Automatic dispatch is blocked with a safe actionable reason. Correcting contact data is distinct from verifying the destination and enabling the purpose/channel. Do not bypass a restriction through another channel. |
| P04 | The same address or telephone number appears on several patient records. | No patient identity is inferred from that value. Each request remains bound to its intended patient/representative and explicit authority. Destination-level suppression applies where required without exposing another patient's records. |
| P05 | Consent to electronic signing exists but communication permission is absent. | Signing consent does not authorize notifications. Purpose/channel/confidentiality choices are evaluated independently, with paper/assisted alternatives available through the supported workflow. |
| P06 | Patient withdraws email or SMS preference while an intent waits in the queue. | Suppress eligible pending delivery. Record effective time and reason. An already accepted external message cannot be recalled; show that boundary honestly rather than claiming suppression succeeded retroactively. |
| P07 | Receive SMS opt-out, re-enablement or help keyword through the selected provider. | Authenticate and deduplicate ingress, apply the provider/sender/destination suppression rules durably, and prevent new eligible messages after opt-out. Staff cannot override it using Retry. A new request or signing consent does not silently clear it. Country-specific keyword and consent policy must be approved before activation. |
| P08 | Contact, intended representative, or authority changes after queueing. | An immutable recipient/destination snapshot prevents silent redirection. Suppress stale request delivery and use an explicit authorized correction/reissue. Completed-copy repair still binds the same exact released version and current authorized recipient. |
| P09 | A request expires, is cancelled, declined, completed, replaced or superseded before reminder dispatch. | Cancel/suppress the stale reminder after a current domain check. Do not reopen the request or rotate it back to active because a delayed callback arrives. Explicit reissue revokes old live grants according to policy. |
| P10 | A reminder is requested twice, after a double click or transport timeout. | Same command returns the same safe operation. Durable intent uniqueness, cooldown and rate limits prevent accidental duplicate messages. An explicit additional send creates a new authorized attempt rather than rewriting the prior attempt. |
| P11 | Multiple incomplete requests are due for one patient; repeated reminder schedulers run. | Apply approved reminder frequency and destination limits across eligible work. Never send a burst per scheduler tick. Preserve each purpose/source in the activity history even if presentation is grouped. No automatic channel fallback without explicit policy and current permission. |
| P12 | Quiet hours, an unknown patient zone or daylight-saving transition changes send time. | Use the documented IANA zone and stated fallback. Persist the next eligible instant, show the recipient-local time, and avoid duplicate or skipped sends when clocks change. Urgent/emergency clinical messaging is outside this workflow. |
| P13 | A message is forwarded, previewed on a lock screen or inspected by an email security scanner. | Default subject/body has no patient name, diagnosis, answers, document category, signature or attachment; use a fixed first-party route. GET does not consume a grant, accept consent, mark read or finish work. Patient verification/session exchange and exact current resource authorization remain required. |
| P14 | A link is expired/revoked, the patient account has no grant, or a proxy loses authority. | Show a generic safe failure and appropriate assistance action. No existence leak, arbitrary redirect, arbitrary patient-ID lookup or email-matching discovery. Never expose staff APIs through patient sessions. |
| P15 | Only the patient signature is received, finalization is processing, output fails, or provider signature is still outstanding. | Do not queue “paperwork ready.” Show signature received, processing or remaining signer status accurately. Completion-only notice is created after the exact durable publication commit. |
| P16 | A form-only workflow is submitted, or completed paperwork is later amended. | Say submitted/ready without a signed claim for form-only copies. Amendment creates a new exact-version release/notice; the old notice never redirects silently to the new version. |
| P17 | The provider times out after possibly accepting the message. | Persist unknown, reconcile/query with a stable provider idempotency key where supported, and block an uncontrolled resend. Job retry is distinct from a new actual provider attempt. Never promise exactly-once email/SMS. |
| P18 | Verified provider callbacks are duplicated, reordered, delayed, unmatched or report delivery followed by bounce/complaint. | Append facts with source/receipt time, deduplicate by provider account/event identity, and apply explicit projections. Preserve late negative facts, suppress future eligible attempts and surface follow-up. Unmatched events cannot assert patient, practice or location scope. |
| P19 | Provider says delivered, tracking reports an open/click, or the app serves document bytes. | Labels remain provider-reported delivery, observed app access or transfer where supported. None becomes proof the person read, understood, completed or accepted paperwork. Disable pixels and link rewriting by default. |
| P20 | Patient locale changes after the document is frozen. | Notification/interface language can change to an approved translation. The frozen signed document/legal language and version remain unchanged. Unsupported translation offers a supported alternative, not an invented translation. |

## Internal staff acceptance

For this design, **ownership change means ownership of workflow work**: assignment, reassignment and removal of the assigned owner. Practice-ownership transfer is a separate administrative security event with a separate audience and policy.

| ID | Scenario | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S01 | A work item is first assigned, reassigned, self-assigned or unassigned. | The new eligible owner receives an actionable notification; the previous owner receives an accurate ownership-change record when appropriate. Avoid duplicate notices to a user filling multiple recipient roles. Self-action may suppress an interruptive alert while preserving source history. Never notify the entire practice by default. |
| S02 | A staff member is suspended, removed or loses the relevant location/permission. | Open work reconciles to unassigned without deleting historical attribution. Existing notification previews/actions and future fanout use current rights; cached content, deep links and idempotent replay cannot recover access. Managers require explicit authorized location access. |
| S03 | A user has several locations or changes active location while opening a notification. | Notifications show the owning location. Opening either confirms/switches to that authorized location or explains unavailable access; it never reads a patient under the wrong location context. Cross-location totals include only current authorized locations. |
| S04 | Work becomes overdue, remains overdue through many scheduler ticks, then its due time changes. | One notice per work item/due generation/threshold/recipient. Rescheduling invalidates stale scheduled alerts. Re-crossing a newly approved due threshold may create a new generation; it does not mutate old history. |
| S05 | Completion, cancellation, waiver, reassignment or due-date change races with overdue fanout. | Recheck domain state and current eligible recipient before dispatch. Suppress obsolete pending alerts. Already-visible records show the current outcome/owner and explain why action is no longer needed. A delayed alert cannot reopen work. |
| S06 | No assignee exists when work becomes overdue. | Route to the configured, eligible location supervisor/queue, or show an explicit unassigned-work exception. Never select a practice owner or broad staff group without current clinical access. Escalation has a finite audience, thresholds and dedupe key. |
| S07 | A user opens, marks read, marks unread or dismisses a notification. | Only that user's attention state changes. The work item remains open until an authorized domain action completes it. Resolved/no-longer-actionable is a separate currentness projection; it does not prove the user read it. |
| S08 | Source work is resolved before a notification is read. | Badge/unread behavior follows the documented product rule, but the row clearly says resolved and its primary action does not invite obsolete work. Notification history remains available under current rights. |
| S09 | “Mark all read” races with incoming notifications, pagination, other tabs or devices. | Update only the user's explicit cutoff/snapshot, not later arrivals. Per-recipient state is idempotent and syncs across devices. Count and list apply the same authorized filters and scope. |
| S10 | Rapid reassignments or a provider outage creates hundreds of related events. | Group presentation by source and current relevance, preserve underlying events, bound visible items and send frequency, and avoid a toast/badge storm. Digest or quiet-hour settings change interruption timing; they do not remove work from Today. |
| S11 | A notification points to deleted/archived source data, unavailable service, or lost rights. | Distinguish safe unavailable, temporary retry and access-removed states without protected previews. Navigation failure never automatically marks work complete or grants rights. |
| S12 | Staff opts out of optional email/push, denies OS permission, or works offline. | In-app workflow remains the baseline. Do not repeatedly request permission. Native push and external staff email must be explicitly enabled and policy-approved; payloads remain generic on lock screens. Local OS dismissal is not server read state. |

## Reliability, security and review gates

| ID | Scenario | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R01 | Crash between domain mutation, audit, outbox enqueue, provider reservation, external acceptance and result persistence. | Domain intent/outbox/audit commit together; leases and fencing prevent stale workers from finalizing. Resume from durable state, reconcile the external boundary, and preserve each actual attempt. No external provider call occurs while pretending a database transaction makes it atomic. |
| R02 | Try cross-practice, cross-location, same-location wrong-patient and wrong-recipient substitutions on commands, jobs, callbacks and list counts. | Deny under the real non-owner runtime role, with safe durable audit. Composite references and current rights protect reads, counts and actions alike. Work assignment and notification creation cannot confer access. |
| R03 | Inspect logs, exports, browser cache, analytics, retry records, push payloads and provider errors. | No bearer token, raw provider credential, patient answers/signatures, arbitrary request body or PHI-bearing URL. Contacts are masked in ordinary staff surfaces; full recipient inspection requires the relevant permission. No PHI in persistent browser cache. |
| R04 | Notification service is down while source work is updated. | Source work remains durable and visible in Today. Retry fanout safely from outbox; display accurate notification availability. Source-domain operations do not silently fail because an optional interruption channel is disabled. |
| R05 | Keyboard/screen reader, 200% text, small phones, touch, dark host and slow connection. | White product surface and approved components remain legible; no horizontal overflow or obscured CTA. Focus returns after dialogs, errors are announced, menus work by keyboard, controls have labels, statuses do not depend on color, and pending actions prevent accidental repeats without losing input. |
| R06 | Review delivery health and incident follow-up. | Authorized operators can see safe queue latency, unknown/failed count, suppression reasons and webhook failures by approved scope; no patient details in metric labels. Retry and recipient repair require distinct permissions and audit. Sandbox/provider tests use synthetic recipients only. |

Before a production pilot, select and approve the email/SMS provider, supported countries, sender identity/domain configuration, patient and proxy assurance, purpose/channel preference model, SMS opt-out behavior, quiet-hour/frequency rules, staff escalation audience, operational permissions and applicable retention policies. Verify agreements and controls under the actual provider relationship. These activation decisions do not block review of the designs and are not satisfied by a successful mockup.
