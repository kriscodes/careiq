## 2. Domain boundaries and defaults

Four independent facts remain separate: request/document workflow state, intent to communicate, provider delivery evidence, and staff attention/read state. Sending failure cannot make a signed copy unsigned, close an open request or make paperwork unavailable. Provider delivery cannot complete a task, prove reading or establish patient identity.

Patient launch message types are request, reminder, reissue and completed-paperwork availability. No marketing, clinical advice, freeform outbound composer, routine attachment or patient SMS conversation is introduced. Staff launch types are assignment, owner change, overdue and delivery follow-up. In-app staff inbox is the primary surface; staff email and native push are preference-controlled channels using minimal content and authenticated deep links. Native push requires its own device registration/provider acceptance before activation.

The sending adapter is shared infrastructure, while patient and staff subjects retain distinct constrained models. Do not remove patient FKs from `delivery_attempts` merely to squeeze staff alerts into that table.

Proposed initial defaults, configurable as reviewed policy rather than hard-coded clinical rules:

- Patient automatic reminders are off until explicitly enabled for a location. A starting policy has slots at 48 hours and 24 hours before due time, at least 24 hours between automatic reminders across channels, a maximum of two automatic reminders over the request's entire lifetime including reissues, a one-hour manual reminder cooldown, and a combined initial/reminder/resend cap of three messages per request/destination in a rolling 24 hours. Reissuing or switching channels cannot reset the automatic reminder allowance. Manual actions cannot bypass channel suppression or caps. If due time is absent, skip due-relative slots. An initial message sent inside a reminder window consumes that slot. These operational numbers require owner review before activation.
- Patient automated delivery runs only from 09:00 through 18:00 in an explicitly confirmed recipient timezone. An unknown/unconfirmed timezone holds automation for staff review; there is no silent location-timezone fallback. Window deferral never extends a request's expiry or sends after a slot becomes obsolete. DST gaps move to the next valid permitted instant; repeated local times resolve once and preserve the policy/zone calculation.
- Staff self-assignment updates the actor's work immediately but produces no external self-alert. Other-owner assignment creates one inbox event for the new owner; reassignment creates a distinct changed-owner event for the previous owner. Unassignment routes to the authorized location manager/fallback queue. No mass alert to every member.
- Overdue is a current property of active open/in-progress work with a nonnull `due_at < now()`, not a new task state. First threshold is due time; a single configured escalation may follow after one business day. There is no periodic duplicate alert while unchanged. Office calendars must be explicit if business-day escalation is enabled.
- Staff external notifications have proposed quiet hours 18:00–08:00 in the confirmed staff preference timezone, with the optional daily digest at 08:30. In-app work remains visible during quiet hours. Escalation after one business day requires an explicit working-day/holiday calendar and the configured authorized escalation audience.

## 3. Event and recipient contract

The authoritative domain command appends its business audit, mutation and outbox event atomically. Event names describe committed facts. Worker retries do not emit a new business fact. Each event carries ID, schema version, causation/operation IDs, actor or service executor, verified practice/location, aggregate type/ID/revision and minimal typed identifiers. No destination, answers, signature, note, bearer token or rendered message body goes in ordinary outbox payloads.

| Event | Producer and idempotent audience | Stops or supersedes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `patient.request.delivery_requested.v1` | Committed request/generation and explicitly selected endpoint/channel; one initial intent per generation/channel | Cancelled/completed/expired/declined request, revoked recipient, changed destination/generation |
| `patient.request.reminder_due.v1` | Durable scheduler or authorized manual reminder; slot/due revision + generation + endpoint/channel | No remaining obligation, terminal request, expiry, cooldown/cap, newer due version |
| `patient.request.reissued.v1` | Recipient correction or explicit reissue command; new grant generation and new reviewed destination | Older queued intents/grants suppressed and sessions revoked according to existing reissue semantics |
| `paperwork.notification.requested` existing | Protected durable completion; exact notification/library item/document version and verified portal principal | Release restricted/voided/superseded, grant or permission lost; library remains durable |
| `work.assignment.changed.v1` | Committed work ownership delta with old/new owner; audience decided once from explicit rules | No-op assignment emits no event; failed/stale command emits none |
| `work.overdue.threshold_crossed.v1` | Scheduler rechecks current active work and its due generation; owner plus escalation audience | Completion/cancellation/reschedule/reassignment makes old dispatch obsolete |
| `patient.delivery.attention_required.v1` | First transition to actionable unknown/permanent failure/suppression requiring staff work | Dedupe by logical intent/problem episode; resolved episode closes only matching follow-up |

Initial producers should be `work_items` and `document_requests` ownership plus existing delivery follow-up work. Insurance or future claim ownership integrates through a reviewed typed adapter with its own current-state/permission/due checks; a generic table-name/JSON event supplied by the browser is prohibited. A registry translates each supported subject into permission, scope, current owner, due revision, terminal states and an allowlisted route.

## 5. Dispatch protocol and state machines

Intent projection: `scheduled → queued → submitting → accepted → delivered`; pre-send alternatives are `suppressed` or `cancelled`; failed calls yield `retry_wait`, `failed` or `unknown`. `needs_attention` is a user-facing aggregate of actionable failures, not proof of a call. Existing API consumers may continue receiving their supported legacy enum plus new explicit reason/next-action fields until versioned contracts adopt the richer projection.

Attempt projection retains existing `queued/submitting/accepted/delivered/failed/unknown`. Add normalized failure class and append facts rather than force every transient step into that enum. Accepted means the provider accepted a message, not that a recipient received it. Delivered is a provider delivery assertion, not opened/read/signed. Email open pixels and tracked/re-written links stay disabled. A late bounce/complaint can make the effective communication outcome failed/attention after delivery while preserving the historical `delivery_confirmed` fact and time. Never simply discard a late terminal fact because a "delivered" enum was considered final.

Dispatch sequence:

1. A domain transaction validates authority/revision, inserts one intent/schedule occurrence, audit and outbox. Return `202 queued` with status link only after durable commit. The protected completion helper continues publishing a completed copy in the same transaction as its completion-notification intent. No real provider is called inside that transaction.
2. A scoped worker claims a bounded outbox batch using row locking/`SKIP LOCKED`, lease expiry and fencing token. It records the actual executor and initiator via existing operation/job-run records. It does not impersonate the initiating staff member after revocation.
3. Under a short transaction, reload domain state, current patient/scope, endpoint version, consent/permission, request/grant generation, expiry, suppression, quiet hours, caps, template approval and connection capability. For paperwork also reload exact released library/version, same verified portal principal and current adult-self eligibility. For staff reload recipient membership/location and the subject's current read capability, including the current directory check when required by existing access policy. A background worker cannot reuse an expired staff token or treat a stale local membership row as current provider authority. Directory uncertainty defers external dispatch. Obsolete work is durably suppressed without a provider call.
4. Reserve the immutable payload/attempt and idempotency key, commit, then call the provider outside PostgreSQL locks. There is necessarily a small external side-effect boundary; do not claim that withdrawal after call start can retract an already accepted message. Cancellation/permission change prevents future calls and leaves a truthful started/unknown result for an in-flight one.
5. Persist call result with the current fencing token; an old worker cannot finalize another worker's lease. A late external response still enters append-only result ingestion keyed to its attempt so it can reconcile safely without owning the current lease. Mark outbox published when the dispatch handoff is durably recorded, not when final delivery is assumed.
6. Reconcile status asynchronously until a supported terminal fact or configured investigation deadline. Resolve/create only the follow-up episode for this intent. Provider outage pauses the connection and produces one aggregated operational alert, not a patient follow-up storm for every retry tick.

Secure request-link handoff needs an implementation change. Generate a fresh request-bound grant for the approved message generation and persist only its verifier in the access table. Put the raw link secret in a short-lived sealed payload accessible only to the dispatch worker, referenced by ID from outbox. Encrypt with an approved secret mechanism, bind ciphertext to intent/request/generation/destination, redact it from audit/logs/traces, and remove it after the provider's safe retry/reconciliation window. A retry of the same external request must use the same message and idempotency key. A fresh generation must not be silently created while the previous outcome is unknown.

Reminders must not depend on a previously consumed link. Issue a new restricted grant within the same intended recipient/request generation for a reminder; keep at most a bounded number of active grants, expire no later than the request, and do not revoke an active patient's session merely because a reminder is due. Explicit reissue or recipient correction retains existing stronger revoke-and-replace behavior. GET/HEAD and link scanners never consume a grant; explicit safe continuation uses the patient exchange POST. Links never contain patient IDs, document titles or direct object URLs. Completed-paperwork notices link to the first-party authenticated library and require current explicit portal access; they do not turn a request bearer token into patient-wide access.

## 6. Retry, callbacks and reconciliation

Retry policy is capability-driven. A bounded starting proposal is five attempts across 24 hours with exponential backoff and jitter, honoring provider `Retry-After`, quiet hours, overall expiry and per-destination caps. Rate limiting and known pre-acceptance transient rejection may retry. Invalid destination, opt-out, complaint, permanent rejection and absent authority suppress/fail without automatic retry.

On timeout, network interruption after write, worker death at the call boundary or response persistence failure, record `unknown`. First query by stable provider idempotency key/message reference. If the provider guarantees deduplication for the whole retry period, the same immutable request/key may be retried. Without lookup or a sufficient dedupe guarantee, retain unknown and require staff/operator reconciliation; do not automatically mint a new key. Once the provider's dedupe window expires, automatic resubmission is unsafe. Exactly-once external delivery is not promised.

Provider documentation examples, checked October 18, 2026, support the distinctions above; no provider is selected or approved by this design. [Twilio's outbound status guide](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/guides/outbound-message-status-in-status-callbacks) separates initial accepted/queued state, sending, and delivered/undelivered results; it also notes that an initial status is obtained from the creation response rather than an initial callback. [Resend's idempotency guide](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys) describes a 24-hour key-retention window for supported email endpoints. The inference for CareIQ is to persist the initial provider response, model acceptance separately from delivery, and configure safe retries to the actual selected adapter's documented capabilities and retention window. These examples do not imply that an arbitrary SMS or email endpoint has equivalent idempotency guarantees.

Callbacks enter an allowlisted provider route with a strict body bound. Validate raw-body signature, supported key, timestamp/replay policy and account before trusting event data. Persist the verified receipt durably before a 2xx acknowledgment. Invalid signatures cannot mutate clinical state and go through bounded platform security evidence without attacker-claimed tenant authority. Event replay/dedup is by connection/account/event; an event with changed hash is quarantined. Unknown provider messages remain unmatched for operational reconciliation, not attached to a UUID supplied in callback metadata. A callback that races ahead of the HTTP response is stored and replayed once the stable provider request/message mapping is durably attached; it is never discarded solely because the response has not arrived.

Append every verified event. Reduce effective outcome using event semantics and provider occurrence time, never blindly apply arrival order. An accepted callback cannot regress a delivered fact. Delivery followed by bounce/complaint preserves both and suppresses later automatic sends. A provider status query appends a reconciliation fact, not a forged callback. Ambiguous/mismatched account/message pairs are quarantined. No callback returns patient details.

Inbound SMS scope is limited to provider opt-out/help handling and nonclinical reply routing. STOP/START handling must update suppression according to the approved provider/sending-identity contract; staff cannot toggle it away. Other replies create one safe staff follow-up with protected content only if inbound reply storage is intentionally implemented and permissioned; otherwise the configured automatic response directs patients to the practice contact. Do not imply two-way texting exists from an outbound implementation.

## 7. Staff inbox, ownership and overdue behavior

Create inbox rows transactionally with the authoritative ownership event or with a deduplicated fan-out step. Same source event delivered twice creates no duplicate. Resolve audience from trusted current assignments; the browser cannot choose arbitrary recipients. Notifications refer to current subject authority. A previously authorized recipient losing access must not retain patient-bearing inbox text, a useful deep link, an unread badge count or a live stream containing that subject.

For a reassignment, old-owner event says the work moved and resolves the previous assignment-attention episode; new-owner event requires attention. Suppress external self-alerts. If the prior owner has lost location access, omit its patient-bearing row externally and expose at most a nonclinical account message when product requirements call for one. A new owner receives current context once; do not replay the entire old owner's history. Acknowledgment, read, archive and snooze never change the underlying work assignment or completion.

Overdue scheduler runs bounded queries using a service scope and explicit current DB time. Lock or conditional-update the work's due-generation checkpoint; emit once for work/due generation/threshold/recipient. Recheck current state, owner and due time immediately before external send. Completion or reschedule resolves the inbox attention; later delivery of an old event cannot resurrect it. Assignment while already overdue creates the new owner's current assignment/overdue episode according to one grouping rule rather than two competing pushes. Managers receive only authorized threshold escalations and unassigned work; there is no default practice-wide broadcast.

Use cursor polling first, with an authenticated event stream later if needed. Push/stream messages carry invalidation/event IDs and a new count only after scoped authorization. Browsers reload authorized detail. External email/push says "You have a new CareIQ work update" with a first-party sign-in link and no patient name, appointment reason, form title or overdue financial facts. Notification access is audited through existing protected access conventions; OS delivery is not evidence that staff read it.

Queue scope is `selected_location` with one explicit currently permitted `locationId`, or `all_permitted_locations` resolved by the server. Cursor signatures bind actor, scope snapshot, filters, sort and cutoff; reject cursor reuse with another patient, status or location. Use a bounded page size (default 50, maximum 100). Counts are explicitly timestamped `asOf` from the same transaction as the page; new refreshes create a new snapshot. A cutoff excludes new intents, while mutable status can change on later pages, so the UI refreshes queue counts rather than treating an old count as a permanent total. Every status/detail/action rechecks current rights. A scope change clears queue details and cursor state. Retry/resend actions remain the exact proposed clinical routes `/api/v1/locations/{locationId}/workflow/deliveries/{intentId}/retry` and `/resend`; they use the stored intent's location and cannot be sent to an ambiguous all-locations mutation target.

Reconcile can update evidence only through authenticated provider lookup/receipt ingestion; the command itself cannot assert a delivery result. Cancel and worker submission use one serialization point, so either cancellation wins before call start or the UI receives a conflict and refreshes truthful current state. A provider cancel facility, if later supported, is a separate adapter capability and not implied by local pre-dispatch cancellation.

Read-all is bounded independently of display pagination. The inbox issues a short-lived snapshot of matching authorized IDs and exact unread count under one database read; use a reviewed cap such as 10,000 IDs and ask for a narrower filter if exceeded. A timestamp alone is insufficient because a fan-out transaction can commit later with an older event time. A signed continuation cutoff backed by a materialized membership set, or an equivalent proven commit-visibility mechanism, is required. Replaying one idempotency key returns the same authorized receipt and never adds alerts that arrived afterward. Read-all does not archive, resolve, assign or complete work. Verify the supplied evidence reference belongs to that same patient/scope and the actor may read it. ### Request reissue and completed-copy recipient repair are different protocols

Existing request reissue accepts only `reasonCode`, revokes the request's access grants and sessions, creates a fresh request-only grant and records a delivery attempt. It does not presently edit the recipient or accept a replacement endpoint. Any proposed recipient-correction payload therefore requires explicit schema/API work and a reviewed new request generation; it cannot be implemented by silently broadening the existing body. The intended person's authority must be confirmed independently of address equality, and all old queued bearer messages are suppressed. Source: [workflow.service.ts:560](/Users/kristian/Documents/Server/CareIQ/apps/api/src/services/workflow.service.ts:560).

Completed paperwork already exists as an exact immutable library item. Existing notification retry looks up only `recipient_grant_id` and reevaluates it; a new verified grant does not automatically repair that pinned notification. Existing portal-grant identity, verification and destination fields are immutable; the source uses revoke-and-create rather than updating the verified binding in place. Sources: [patient-paperwork.service.ts:79](/Users/kristian/Documents/Server/CareIQ/apps/api/src/services/patient-paperwork.service.ts:79), [0028_patient-paperwork.sql:75](/Users/kristian/Documents/Server/CareIQ/apps/api/drizzle/0028_patient-paperwork.sql:75).

The proposed repair command must require current notification revision, a reason, exact replacement grant/endpoint version, same patient/practice/location and the same independently verified authorized portal principal. It records old/new grant references in protected audit, rechecks current release/eligibility/preferences, suppresses any stale queued message and creates a new intent under the same completion notification/library/version. It does not rotate or revive request signing tokens, repeat completion, or silently bind a different account. A different portal principal requires the separate authorized portal-binding/access decision; notification retry is never an identity-transfer mechanism. An unknown prior send must reconcile before creating a second external message. This repair protocol is proposed; the current source does not implement it.

## 10. Required acceptance evidence

These are implementation requirements, not tests run for this document.

- Domain mutation, audit and one logical outbox/intent either commit together or roll back. Duplicate HTTP commands, event fan-out and scheduler scans produce one logical result after transport-key expiry. Same key/different body conflicts.
- Two workers, lease expiry and a crash before call, after provider acceptance and before DB result commit preserve a truthful state and prevent unsafe resending. Old fencing token cannot finalize current state; provider fact ingestion can still reconcile a late response.
- Invalid, duplicate, reordered, cross-account, unmatched and same-ID/different-hash callbacks cannot cross tenant/patient boundaries or fabricate delivery. Late bounce/complaint suppresses future sends without erasing earlier delivery facts.
- A destination/recipient/consent change after queueing suppresses the stale secret-bound intent. Request correction reissues explicitly. A worker never substitutes a new patient contact for the old bearer message silently.
- Initial request, reminder and reissue remain open independent of provider outage; a consumed request link does not make the next reminder unusable; link scanner GET cannot consume or prove access. Raw message tokens never appear in ordinary outbox/audit/logs or status responses.
- No message is sent after request completion/cancellation/expiry or when its obligation disappears. Concurrent due/owner changes, DST and quiet-hour deferral consume each valid slot once and skip obsolete slots. Caps include manual sends.
- Both signed and unsigned submitted copies publish exact immutable bytes before one completion intent. Absent permission, failed provider, grant expiry/revocation, archive, lost adult-self eligibility and release restrictions leave the durable library fact intact and give truthful follow-up.
- Normal retry cannot override a suppressed destination, rebind portal identity, advance expired consent or duplicate an unknown send. A repaired grant is selected through an explicit authorized same-patient/principal path.
- Staff assignment/no-op/reassignment/unassignment/self-assignment produces the specified audience once. Revoked staff cannot retain details/counts/pushes/streams; deep links reauthorize. Read/archive/snooze never complete work.
- Overdue dedup covers work/due generation/threshold/recipient; completion/reschedule/reassignment immediately before dispatch suppresses stale external delivery and resolves stale attention. No unchanged-state notification storm occurs.
- EN/ES template rendering, minimal external payload, masked destinations, inaccessible-subject behavior, accessible inbox counts and expired/failed links match the product designs. Provider acceptance never appears as delivered/read.
- Migration replay, existing workflow/paperwork API consumers, RLS/grants and audit-reader isolation continue passing. Live provider and installed-device results are separately recorded; a synthetic adapter or screenshot cannot satisfy those activation gates.
